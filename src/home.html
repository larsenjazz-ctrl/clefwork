    Clefwork The quiz builder for the basics: placing and naming notes, scale degrees, intervals, chords, scales and key signatures. Open Clefwork → Clefwork Harmony Progressions, figured bass and chord voicings — and cadences and modulations: students write the chords in four parts, name the cadence or modulation, or both. Open Harmony → Clefwork Keys Note reading three ways — on the grand staff, as a name with its octave, and on the piano keyboard, which plays each note. Open Keys → Clefwork Analysis Box the chords on a picture of real music; students write Roman numerals, chord symbols, or both. Open Analysis → Clefwork Rhythm Rhythmic dictation: students hear a rhythm in one or two parts and write it on a one-line staff. Open Rhythm → Clefwork Melody Melodic dictation: students hear a melody in a major or minor key and write its pitches and rhythm on the staff. Open Melody → Clefwork Sight Singing Students read a melody, hear Do and the scale, then record themselves singing it and hand the recording in. Open Sight Singing → Clefwork Chord Graph The chord graph as worksheets: diatonic progression tables, tritone substitution graphs, and phrases in Roman numerals checked against the graph’s rules. Open Chord Graph → Clefwork Terms Musical terms, multiple choice: dynamics, tempo markings and instrument families — and hearing the dynamics and tempo of a melody. Open Terms → Clefwork Voice Leading Block chords: students write a block voicing for each chord symbol on the grand staff. Every chord is marked by the textbook’s rules, and you can accept or reject any mark. Open Voice Leading →
